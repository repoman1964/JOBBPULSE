# JobPulse Product Requirements Document

**Product:** JobPulse  
**Document type:** Product Requirements Document  
**Status:** Working PRD  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Primary audience:** Founder, product, design and engineering  
**Initial market:** Visual home-service contractors  
**Target subscription range:** $197–$297 per month  

---

## 1. Product Summary

JobPulse is a contractor-first marketing automation platform for visual home-service businesses.

The contractor creates a **Job**, captures before-and-after photos, records a short voice description of the work completed and submits those materials to JobPulse. The system uses the photos and voice description to generate authentic marketing content based on the actual job.

The contractor reviews the generated content, approves or rejects it and continues revising or regenerating it until satisfied. Once approved, JobPulse publishes the content through a third-party distribution platform to the contractor’s connected social media accounts and also publishes the completed-job content to a locally focused web directory owned and controlled by JobPulse.

The core promise is:

> Finish the job. Show JobPulse what you did. JobPulse turns it into marketing.

---

## 2. Product Vision

JobPulse will become the marketing layer that sits directly on top of a contractor’s completed work.

Every completed job can become:

- Social media content
- Before-and-after proof
- A permanent local project listing
- Searchable evidence of the contractor’s work
- Educational content
- Trust-building content
- Future advertising creative
- A reusable marketing asset

The long-term product loop is:

**Create a Job → capture the work → generate content → human approval → distribute content → build local visibility → generate inquiries → complete more jobs**

JobPulse is not primarily a social media scheduler. Its wedge is converting real completed work into marketing with minimal effort from the contractor.

---

## 3. Strategic Principle: Ownership and Control

JobPulse will not depend on automated posting to local Facebook groups.

Automating activity inside Facebook groups can put a contractor’s personal or business account at risk. It also forces JobPulse to build a core distribution strategy on a third-party platform that JobPulse does not control.

Instead, JobPulse will create and operate its own locally focused web directory.

The directory is a core strategic asset because JobPulse controls:

- The content format
- The publishing rules
- The contractor profiles
- The project pages
- The local taxonomy
- The lead flow
- The search experience
- The long-term data
- The platform roadmap
- The business model

The owned directory is not a side feature. It is one of the two primary destinations for approved content.

The two content destinations are:

1. The contractor’s connected social media platforms
2. The JobPulse-owned local web directory

## 4. Problem Statement

Visual home-service contractors regularly complete work that could help them win future customers but fail to turn that work into consistent marketing.

Common problems include:

- Before-and-after photos remain on a phone
- Contractors forget to post after finishing a job
- Contractors do not know what to write
- Social media posting is inconsistent
- Existing posts are generic
- Owners do not have time to create content
- Marketing agencies are too expensive
- Social media tools still require the user to create the content first
- Valuable proof of work disappears quickly in social feeds
- Contractors lack a permanent local destination for completed projects

Most social media products begin after the content already exists. JobPulse begins at the jobsite.

## 5. Target Customer

### 5.1 Primary Customer Profile

The initial customer is an owner-operated or small-to-midsized visual home-service company.

Typical characteristics:

- Serves a local or regional market
- Completes visually demonstrable work
- Has no full-time marketing team
- Takes job photos with a smartphone
- Posts inconsistently
- Relies heavily on referrals and local reputation
- Has an average job value of at least approximately $500
- Ideally has an average job value of $1,000 or more
- Can justify the monthly subscription from one additional booked job

### 5.2 Initial Niche Rollout

#### Phase 1

1. Painting
2. Tree service and stump removal
3. Hardscaping and landscape installation

#### Phase 2

4. Flooring
5. Fencing and decks
6. Roofing

### 5.3 Niche Selection Criteria

A strong JobPulse niche should have:

- Strong before-and-after visuals
- Clear project completion points
- Jobs valuable enough to support a $197–$297 monthly subscription
- Frequent opportunities to capture work
- Local demand
- A strong need for trust and proof
- Owners who are not consistent marketers

Job economics and visual proof matter more than raw appointment frequency.

The primary user is a contractor who is involved in both operations and growth.

The user may:

## 7. Core Product Object: The Job

The central object in JobPulse is a **Job**.

A Job represents a real project completed for a customer.

Each Job should contain:

- Job title or service type
- General location
- Before photos
- After photos
- Optional progress photos
- Voice description
- Transcript
- Structured job details extracted by AI
- Generated content
- Approval status
- Publishing status
- Directory listing status
- Privacy settings

The Job is the source record from which all content is created.

## 8. Core Workflow

### Step 1: Create a Job

The contractor creates a new Job in JobPulse.

Creating a Job must be extremely easy and should require as little typing as possible.

At minimum, the contractor should be able to:

- Tap **Create Job**
- Add a service type or short title
- Capture or upload photos
- Save the Job and return later

The contractor may create the Job before work begins so the before photos are stored immediately.

### Step 2: Add Before Photos

The contractor captures photos before starting the work.

The MVP must support at least three stored photos per Job.

The photo experience should encourage a clear before-and-after story.

The product should allow photos to be labeled as:

- Before
- Progress
- After

At least one before photo and one after photo should be strongly encouraged before content generation.

### Step 3: Complete the Work

The contractor or crew performs the job.

The Job remains saved and accessible throughout the work.

### Step 4: Add After Photos

After the work is completed, the contractor returns to the existing Job and adds after photos.

The system should make it obvious that the user is completing an existing Job rather than creating another record.

### Step 5: Add a Voice Description

The contractor records a short voice summary explaining:

- What the customer needed
- What work was completed
- What changed
- What materials or techniques were used
- What was difficult or unusual
- What result was achieved
- Where the work was completed, at a safe local level
- Any homeowner advice worth sharing

Voice is preferred over typing because it is faster and more natural for contractors.

### Step 6: Submit for Generation

The contractor submits the completed Job.

JobPulse processes:

- Before photos
- After photos
- Optional progress photos
- Voice recording
- Transcript
- Basic Job data
- Contractor profile and brand settings

### Step 7: Generate Content

JobPulse creates content grounded in the actual Job.

Potential outputs include:

- Main social media post
- Short caption
- Before-and-after post
- Educational post
- Project summary
- Directory project listing
- Suggested call to action
- Suggested hashtags
- Suggested project title

### Step 8: Human Review Loop

The contractor reviews the generated content.

The contractor can:

- Approve
- Reject
- Edit
- Regenerate
- Change tone
- Request a shorter version
- Request a longer version
- Remove details
- Add missing details
- Change selected photos

The review loop continues until the contractor is satisfied.

No content is distributed until the contractor approves it.

### Step 9: Publish Approved Content

Once approved, the content is sent to two destinations.

#### Destination A: Social Media

JobPulse sends the approved content through a third-party publishing platform such as Blotato or a similar service.

Potential destinations include:

- Facebook business pages
- Instagram
- Google Business Profile, when practical
- LinkedIn, when relevant
- Other supported platforms

#### Destination B: JobPulse Local Directory

The approved Job is also published as a structured project listing in the JobPulse-owned local directory.

The directory listing may include:

- Contractor name
- Service type
- General service area
- Before-and-after images
- Project summary
- Work completed
- Call to action
- Link to contractor profile
- Contact or lead form

## 10. Job Creation Experience

The Job creation experience is one of the highest-priority areas of the product.

The contractor should not feel like they are filling out a CRM form.

### 10.1 Design Goals

Creating a Job should be:

- Fast
- Mobile-first
- Obvious
- Forgiving
- Saveable at any stage
- Easy to resume
- Usable with one hand
- Low in required typing

### 10.2 Suggested Initial Flow

1. Tap **Create Job**
2. Take or upload before photos
3. Add optional service type
4. Tap **Save Job**
5. Return after work is complete
6. Add after photos
7. Record voice summary
8. Tap **Generate Content**

### 10.3 Possible Quick-Create Fields

Required:

- Photos
- Job state
- Company association

Optional at creation:

- Service type
- City or service area
- Short Job title

The system can infer or request missing information later.

## 12. AI Requirements

### 12.1 Job Understanding

The system should extract:

- Service type
- Customer problem
- Work completed
- Materials
- Equipment
- Techniques
- Challenges
- Final result
- General location
- Duration, when stated
- Customer reaction, when stated
- Maintenance advice
- Safety details
- Contractor differentiators

### 12.2 Content Rules

Generated content must:

- Be grounded in submitted information
- Avoid invented details
- Avoid unsupported claims
- Avoid invented measurements or prices
- Avoid publishing exact addresses
- Avoid identifying customers without permission
- Avoid license or certification claims unless verified
- Match the contractor’s preferred tone
- Emphasize the actual transformation
- Sound local and authentic
- Avoid repetitive AI-style language
- Use clear homeowner-friendly language
- Include a useful call to action

### 12.3 Tone Options

Initial tone options may include:

- Straightforward and professional
- Friendly and local
- Expert and educational
- Rugged and direct
- Premium and polished

The system should eventually learn from contractor edits and approvals.

## 23. Key Risks

### Contractors Do Not Create Jobs Consistently

**Mitigation:** Make creation extremely easy, allow early before-photo capture and send useful reminders.

### Contractors Forget to Return After the Work

**Mitigation:** Surface incomplete Jobs, send reminders and make resuming obvious.

### Poor Photo Quality

**Mitigation:** Provide simple photo guidance and warn about unusable images.

### Generic AI Content

**Mitigation:** Ground output in the actual photos, transcript and contractor profile.

### Approval Takes Too Long

**Mitigation:** Keep the review screen simple and provide one-tap regeneration options.

### Publishing Provider Dependency

**Mitigation:** Use a replaceable integration layer.

### Directory Has No Initial Traffic

**Mitigation:** Treat the directory first as a durable contractor asset and local content database, then layer in SEO, discovery and lead generation.

### Privacy Problems

**Mitigation:** Avoid exact addresses, require approval and provide unpublish controls.

### Expanding Too Early

**Mitigation:** Focus on the locked Phase 1 niches before broadening.

The MVP must answer:

3. Is three-photo support sufficient for the initial workflow?
4. Is voice faster and easier than typed descriptions?
5. Will contractors approve AI-generated content?
6. How many revision loops are typically required?
7. Will contractors publish to both social media and the JobPulse directory?
8. Which niche creates the highest volume of publishable Jobs?
9. Which niche retains best?
10. Does the directory improve perceived value?
11. Can one additional booked job justify the monthly fee?
12. Can the workflow remain simple enough for regular field use?

## 26. Final Product Definition

JobPulse is a job-to-marketing platform for visual home-service contractors.

Its core product flow is:

**Create a Job → capture before photos → complete the work → capture after photos → record a voice summary → generate content → human review and revision → approve → publish to social media and the JobPulse-owned local directory**

The product wins by making that flow easy enough for a working contractor to use consistently.
